# Vedic Phonetics Guide — वैदिक शिक्षा

A complete reference for Sanskrit pronunciation, Vedic recitation rules, and the phonetic analysis system used by Vedic Voice Studio.

## The Six Vedāṅgas (Limbs of the Veda)

Sanskrit phonetics falls under **Śīkṣā** (शिक्षा), the first of the six Vedāṅgas:

| Vedāṅga | Sanskrit | Purpose |
|---------|----------|---------|
| **Śīkṣā** | शिक्षा | Phonetics & pronunciation |
| **Chandas** | छन्दः | Prosody & meter |
| **Vyākaraṇa** | व्याकरण | Grammar |
| **Nirukta** | निरुक्त | Etymology |
| **Jyotiṣa** | ज्योतिष | Astronomy/calendar |
| **Kalpa** | कल्प | Ritual procedure |

**Rules:**
- Hrasva (short) vowels = 1 mātrā (1 beat)
- Dīrgha (long) vowels = 2 mātrā (2 beats)
- Pluta (prolonged) = 3 mātrā (rare, used in Vedic calling)

## 2. The Five Mouth Positions (Pañca Sthāna)

### Detailed Articulatory Instructions

#### Kaṇṭhya (Guttural) — कण्ठ्य
- **Location**: Back of the throat / soft palate
- **Instruction**: Pull the back of the tongue toward the soft palate. The airflow originates from the throat.
- **Sounds**: ka, kha, ga, gha, ṅa, ha, a, ā
- **Common error**: Not going far enough back — sounds like a dental

#### Tālavya (Palatal) — तालव्य
- **Location**: Hard palate (roof of the mouth)
- **Instruction**: Press the flat/blade of the tongue against the hard palate. The tongue is wide and flat.
- **Sounds**: ca, cha, ja, jha, ña, ya, śa, i, ī
- **Common error**: Confusing with English 'ch' — Sanskrit ca is softer

#### Mūrdhanya (Retroflex) — मूर्धन्य
- **Location**: Dome of the palate, behind the alveolar ridge
- **Instruction**: **Curl the tongue tip backward** so the underside touches the roof of the mouth. This is the most distinctive Sanskrit position.
- **Sounds**: ṭa, ṭha, ḍa, ḍha, ṇa, ra, ṣa, ṛ, ṝ
- **Common error**: Not curling the tongue far enough — sounds like a dental

#### Dantya (Dental) — दन्त्य
- **Location**: Upper front teeth
- **Instruction**: Press the tongue tip firmly against the **back of the upper front teeth**. NOT the alveolar ridge (as in English t/d).
- **Sounds**: ta, tha, da, dha, na, la, sa, ḷ
- **Common error**: English speakers default to alveolar (English-style t/d), not true dental

#### Oṣṭhya (Labial) — ओष्ठ्य
- **Location**: Both lips
- **Instruction**: Close both lips together. For aspirated sounds, release with a puff.
- **Sounds**: pa, pha, ba, bha, ma, u, ū
- **Common error**: Insufficient lip rounding for u/ū

## 3. Prāṇa — Breath Force (प्राण)

### Alpaprāṇa (Unaspirated) — अल्पप्राण
- **Rule**: Light, controlled breath. No air puff after the consonant.
- **Test**: Hold a feather near your lips — it should NOT move.
- **Sounds**: ka, ga, ca, ja, ṭa, ḍa, ta, da, pa, ba

### Mahāprāṇa (Aspirated) — महाप्राण
- **Rule**: Strong diaphragm-driven breath. A distinct puff of air follows the consonant.
- **Test**: Hold a feather near your lips — it SHOULD move.
- **Sounds**: kha, gha, cha, jha, ṭha, ḍha, tha, dha, pha, bha
- **Instruction**: The aspiration comes from the diaphragm, not just the mouth.

## 4. Svara — The Three-Tone Pitch System (स्वर)

Vedic recitation uses a **three-tone pitch system** that predates musical scales:

### Udātta (उदात्त) — High/Baseline Pitch
- **Notation**: Unmarked (or marked with `ú` above)
- **Instruction**: This is your natural speaking pitch. Maintain a steady, mid-to-high register.
- **Rule**: The "default" tone for most syllables.

### Anudātta (अनुदात्त) — Low Pitch
- **Notation**: Marked with `_` below the syllable
- **Instruction**: Drop your vocal pitch **one clear step below** the udātta baseline.
- **Rule**: Always immediately precedes an udātta, creating the rising contour.

### Svarita (स्वरित) — Falling/Circumflex Pitch
- **Notation**: Marked with `^` above the syllable
- **Instruction**: **Rise above** the udātta pitch, then **cascade back** to baseline within the same syllable. A brief "peak and fall."
- **Rule**: Automatically placed on the syllable immediately **after** an udātta (if that syllable is not itself udātta).

### Pitch Contour Example
```
Syllable:  ta   sa   vi   tur
Svara:     _    ú    ^    _
Pitch:     low  MID  HIGH→mid  low
```

## 5. Special Sounds

### Visarga (विसर्ग) — ḥ
- **Symbol**: ः
- **Rule**: A soft, **passive echo** of the preceding vowel. The mouth stays in the same position as the preceding vowel.
- **Examples**:
  - `devaḥ` → "deva-ha" (soft, short 'h' with 'a' quality)
  - `guruḥ` → "guru-hu" (soft 'h' with 'u' quality)
- **WARNING**: Never pronounce as a harsh, forced "HAA" — this is the most common error.

### Anusvāra (अनुस्वार) — ṁ
- **Symbol**: ं
- **Rule**: Pure **nasal resonance**. The preceding vowel is nasalized. The tongue does NOT touch the palate.
- **Examples**:
  - `saṁskṛtam` → nasalized 'a' followed by 's'
  - `oṁ` → nasalized 'o'
- **Difference from 'n' or 'm'**: Anusvāra is a nasal coloring of the vowel, not a distinct consonant.

### Candrabindu (चन्द्रबिन्दु) — ँ
- **Symbol**: ँ
- **Rule**: Lighter nasalization than anusvāra. Used primarily in Hindi.

### Avagraha (अवग्रह) — ऽ
- **Symbol**: ऽ
- **Rule**: Marks an elided initial 'a' in sandhi. Hold the preceding vowel slightly longer.
- **Example**: `so'ham` = `saḥ + aham` → the initial 'a' of 'aham' is absorbed.

## 6. Sandhi — Euphonic Combination (सन्धि)

### Svara Sandhi (Vowel Sandhi)
| Rule | Example | Result |
|------|---------|--------|
| a/ā + a/ā → ā | mahā + ātmā | mahātmā |
| a/ā + i/ī → e | deva + indra | devendra |
| a/ā + u/ū → o | mahā + utsava | mahotsava |
| a/ā + ṛ → ar | mahā + ṛṣi | maharṣi |
| i/ī + vowel → y + vowel | iti + atra | ityatra |
| u/ū + vowel → v + vowel | madhu + ari | madhvari |

### Visarga Sandhi
| Rule | Example | Result |
|------|---------|--------|
| aḥ + voiced → o | devaḥ + gacchati | devo gacchati |
| aḥ + a → o' | devaḥ + asti | devo'sti |
| āḥ + voiced → ā | devāḥ + gacchanti | devā gacchanti |

### Consonant Sandhi
| Rule | Example | Result |
|------|---------|--------|
| t + voiced → d | tat + dhāma | tad dhāma |
| t + ś → c ch | tat + śiva | tac chiva |
| t + c → c c | tat + ca | tac ca |
| n + ś → ñ ch | tān + śiva | tāñ chiva |

## 7. The Six Faults of Recitation (Ṣaḍ-Doṣa)

These must be **strictly avoided** in Vedic chanting:

| # | Doṣa | Sanskrit | Description | How to Avoid |
|---|-------|----------|-------------|-------------|
| 1 | **Gītī** | गीती | Musical drifting / singing | Maintain steady pitch, don't add melodic ornamentation |
| 2 | **Śīghrī** | शीघ्री | Rushing through syllables | Keep uniform tempo, give each syllable its full mātrā |
| 3 | **Śirakampī** | शिरकम्पी | Head-bobbing to force notes | Keep the head still; pitch changes come from the voice only |
| 4 | **Likhitapāṭhī** | लिखितपाठी | Mechanical reading without feeling | Understand the meaning; let understanding guide delivery |
| 5 | **Ananusvāritā** | अननुस्वारिता | Dropping nasalization | Always pronounce anusvāra with full nasal resonance |
| 6 | **Visvara** | विस्वर | Wrong pitch on marked syllables | Study and practice the svara markings carefully |

## 8. Pronunciation Quick Reference

### Common Mispronunciation Traps

| Sound | Wrong (English habit) | Correct (Sanskrit) |
|-------|--------------------|-------------------|
| त ta | Alveolar 't' (tongue on ridge) | Dental 't' (tongue on teeth) |
| ट ṭa | Same as 't' | Retroflex (tongue curled back) |
| द da | Alveolar 'd' | Dental 'd' (tongue on teeth) |
| श śa | English 'sh' | Palatal sibilant (softer, blade of tongue) |
| ष ṣa | English 'sh' | Retroflex sibilant (tongue curled back) |
| स sa | English 's' | Dental sibilant (tongue on teeth) |
| ह ha | Hard English 'h' | Soft, breathy 'h' from open glottis |
| ऋ ṛ | English 'ri' | Short vocalic 'r' (between 'r' and 'ri') |
| ज्ञ jña | English 'gya' | True pronunciation: 'gnya' or 'dnya' (regional) |

### Retroflex vs Dental Comparison

| Dental (tongue on teeth) | Retroflex (tongue curled back) |
|--------------------------|-------------------------------|
| त ta | ट ṭa |
| थ tha | ठ ṭha |
| द da | ड ḍa |
| ध dha | ढ ḍha |
| न na | ण ṇa |
| स sa | ष ṣa |
| ल la | (no retroflex 'l' in standard Sanskrit) |
